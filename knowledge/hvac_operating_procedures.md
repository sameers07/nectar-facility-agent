# HVAC Operating Procedures

## What is an AHU?

An AHU (Air Handling Unit) circulates and conditions air for a building
zone. It draws in return and/or outside air, passes it through filters and
a cooling or heating coil, and supplies conditioned air through ductwork.
Airflow through an AHU is one of the primary health indicators for the
zone it serves -- a drop in airflow reduces cooling capacity even if the
chiller supplying it is functioning normally.

## What is a chiller?

A chiller removes heat from a water loop (chilled water), which is then
circulated to AHUs to cool the air they supply. Chillers are typically the
single largest energy consumer in a facility's HVAC system, so a rise in
chiller power draw is a common early signal of an inefficiency elsewhere
in the loop, including on the AHU side.

## Startup sequence

On a normal HVAC startup: chillers are started first and allowed to reach
setpoint, chilled water pumps are verified running, then AHUs are enabled
in sequence rather than all at once, to avoid a simultaneous demand spike.

## Building temperature targets

Standard occupied-hours setpoint is 22-24C. A building reporting a
temperature above 26C during occupied hours should be investigated as a
potential HVAC issue rather than assumed to be a thermostat or occupancy
effect.
